Create a 12-slide submission deck for the "Agentic Swarm — The AI Boardroom" hackathon.

AUDIENCE: hackathon judges scoring business value, agent specialisation, agent
collaboration, decision quality, adaptability, technical implementation and presentation.

STYLE: dark background, high contrast, one idea per slide. Points, not paragraphs. Short
lines. Clean diagrams over decoration. Indian rupees written as "INR 27 crore", never with a
currency symbol.

CRITICAL EVIDENCE RULE: every number, percentage and portfolio figure on a slide must come
from the source documents. Do NOT invent statistics, latencies, ID codes, accuracy figures
or decorative metrics. If a slide needs a number and the sources do not supply one, leave it
out rather than inventing it.

SLIDE PLAN — follow this order:

1. TITLE — "The AI Boardroom". Subtitle: "A board that convenes on any business problem".
   Note the track: Theme A — FINSWARM (FinNova Capital, digital small-business lending).

2. THE PROBLEM — FinNova has INR 30 crore, room for 700 loans, three customer segments and
   eight hard limits. The question is not who will default (a credit model answers that) but
   which portfolio to actually write. That question has no single owner.

3. THE TRACK AND THE BOARD — the six mandated agents (Business Research, Finance and
   Treasury, Credit Risk, Marketing and Sales, Compliance and Customer Protection, CEO).
   For each, show what it owns and which hard constraint it can veto. Note that Intake is a
   deterministic pre-board tool, not a judged agent.

4. ARCHITECTURE — the pipeline: brief, intake, case pack, validator, deterministic search,
   the board, decision plus audit trail. Emphasise that no Python file knows what industry
   the problem is; the roster, variables, formulas and constraints all live in a JSON pack.

5. SPECIALISATION IS STRUCTURAL — every variable carries a visible_to list, so Marketing
   literally cannot see the segment default rates. Credit Risk sees 10 of 22 variables. The
   same field doubles as the impact map used on a surprise.

6. NUMBERS FROM A SANDBOX, NOT FROM THE MODEL — agents state lever preferences and 0-1
   signals; a whitelisted Python AST evaluator computes every figure. No attribute access,
   no imports, no builtins, unknown names rejected before compilation.

7. TC1 RESULT — THE GREEDY ANSWER IS ILLEGAL — 450 manufacturers plus 250 SMEs is the
   profit-maximising mix and is arithmetically infeasible: it asks to lend INR 55.5 crore
   against INR 30 crore and puts manufacturers at about 73 percent of the book against a
   70 percent cap. Finance blocked it on liquidity, Credit Risk on concentration.

8. TC1 RESULT — THE FEASIBLE OPTIMUM AND THE ARGUMENT IT STARTED — the chosen portfolio
   G1 (0 retail, 306 SME, 96 manufacturer loans at 19 percent) serves zero retail shops.
   Marketing backed a retail-inclusive alternative instead and filed a material objection;
   Compliance counter-objected that higher retail exposure raises conduct risk. Show this as
   a real disagreement between departments.

9. TC1 CEO DECISION — the one-sentence decision plus the eight mandated sections that are
   validated in code (customer segment, product terms, approval policy, budget allocation,
   risk limits, go-to-market, implementation sequence, measurable outcomes), the rejected
   alternatives with the constraint each broke, and the three KPIs.

10. TC2 SURPRISE — SELECTIVE RE-RUN — defaults jump to 8 / 5 / 7 percent and the committee
    tightens the portfolio cap to 5.5 percent. Compliance, Credit Risk and Finance re-ran;
    Research and Marketing did not, because the changed variables are not visible to them.
    List the four invalidated assumptions.

11. TC2 FINDING — THE PILOT IS INFEASIBLE AS DESIGNED — zero feasible portfolios. The 70
    percent concentration cap forces at least 30 percent of the book into a segment
    defaulting at 7 percent or worse, which breaches the 5.5 percent cap; an all-SME book
    clears the default cap but breaches concentration. The two rules are jointly
    unsatisfiable. That is the answer TC2 asks for: continue, redesign or pause.

12. HOW IT IS BUILT AND WHAT WE CLAIM — Python 3.11, Pydantic, stdlib ast sandbox, stdlib
    random search with local refinement, ThreadPoolExecutor, Gemini 3 Flash, Streamlit. No
    agent framework and no solver, deliberately. Five-rung fallback ladder that never
    raises. Closing honest line: portfolio optimisation under constraints is textbook; the
    contribution is the decision layer on top — constraint ownership, cross-functional
    objection, and selective re-planning, with a full audit trail.
